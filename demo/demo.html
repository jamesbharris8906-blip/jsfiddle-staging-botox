How to Get Ready and What to Do After

Before your visit, tell the team about all the pills, vitamins, and shots you take. Ask if you should skip a blood thinner or a pain pill for a day or two, but only if your own doctor says it is safe. Wear no heavy makeup if you can, and bring a list of questions. After Botox, try not to rub the spots, lie flat, or work out hard for the rest of the day. Skip a hot bath, a sauna, or a long time in the sun that day too. These small steps help the Botox stay where it was placed and help your skin heal in a calm way.

Frequently Asked Questions

When will I see a change after Botox?

Most people start to see a change in 3 to 5 days. The full change often shows up in about 2 weeks. It is slow, so you may wake up one morning and see that the lines look softer than they did the day before.

Does Botox hurt?

Most people say it feels like a tiny pinch that lasts a second. The needle is very fine, and the visit is quick. If you are shy about needles, tell the team. They can talk you through each step and go at your pace.

How long does Botox last?

For most people, Botox lasts about 3 to 4 months. It can last a little less or a little more based on your face, how much was used, and how your body acts. When the lines start to come back, you can book a new visit.

Will my face still move, or will I look frozen?

Your face should still move. The team uses a small amount in the right spots, so you can smile, laugh, and lift your brows. The goal is a soft look, not a stiff face. If you want a very light touch, say so at your visit.

What should I avoid after Botox?

For the rest of the day, try not to rub or press the spots, lie down flat, or do a hard workout. Skip hot baths, saunas, and long sun time that day. You can wash your face with a light touch and go back to most of your normal day.

Who should talk with a doctor first before Botox?

Talk with a doctor first if you are pregnant, nursing, or plan to be soon. Also talk first if you have a nerve or muscle illness, a skin rash or sore where the shot would go, or a past bad reaction to Botox. Bring a list of your pills and health notes to your visit.

Can I get Botox and another treatment on the same day?

Some pairs work well on the same day, and some work best on a new day. It rests on your skin, your health, and the type of treatment. The team will tell you what is safe to pair and what to space out, so you get the best result.
